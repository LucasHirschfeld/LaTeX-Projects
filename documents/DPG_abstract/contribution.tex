\scTitle{Normal Incidence X-ray Standing Wave (NIXSW) technique of Quinacridone on the Ag(100) surface}
\scAuthor{*}{Lucas}{Hirschfeld}{1}
\scAffiliation{1}{Clausius Institute for Physical and Theoretical Chemistry, Universtiy of Bonn, Germany}
\scBeginText

Vacuum deposited films of quinacridone (QA, 5,12-dihydro-quino[2,3-\textit{b}]acridine-7,14-dione) on the Ag(100) surface have been investigated with focus on their ordering via hydrogen bonds and the change of the adsorbed molecule during the phase transition using Normal Incidence X-ray Standing Wave (NIXSW) technique in combination with X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS) performed at the Diamond Light Source in UK.\\ \\
QA on the Ag(100) surface forms an point-on-line structure at room temperature ($\alpha$-phase), which transforms into an commensurate and thermodynamically favored $\beta$-phase upon annealing to 500~K~[1]. The NIXSW and XPS measurements revealed that the phase transition involves a partly deprotonation of the amine group and a decrease of the adsorption height for the complete molecule and especially for the deprotonated nitrogen atoms.\\

[1] Journal of Physical Chemistry C 124.45 (2020), 24861–24873.
