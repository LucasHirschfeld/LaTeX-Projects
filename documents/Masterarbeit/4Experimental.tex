\chapter{Experimental}

\section{The Ag(100) crystal surface}

As substrate for the adsorption of \ac{QA} molecules, a silver crystal with a (100) surface orientation provided by the team of the I09 beamline of the Diamond Light Source is used. Silver crystallizes in a \ac{fcc} structure with a lattice constant of $a_\mathrm{Ag} = 4.0856 \pm 0.0020$~\si{\angstrom} at a temperature of 25~\si{\degreeCelsius}.\autocite{Weast1974, Smakula1955,Straumanis1971} The (100) surface is created by cutting the crystal along the plane defined by the [100] and [010] directions. This results in a square surface unit cell with a lattice constant of $a_\mathrm{Ag(100)} = a_\mathrm{Ag} / \sqrt{2} = 2.8890$~\si{\angstrom}. The unit cell of the Ag(100) surface and the distinct adsorption sites on the Ag(100) surface are illustrated in \autoref{fig:Ag(100)}.

\begin{figure}[H]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{images/vertiefer/Ag(100).pdf}
	\caption{Unit cell of the silver crystal with marked (100) plane and the Ag(100) surface with the unit cell vectors (black arrows) and the distinct adsorption sites (black circles).}
	\label{fig:Ag(100)}
\end{figure}

The Ag(100) crystal was cleaned in ultra-high vacuum (UHV) conditions by repeated cycles of Ar$^+$ ion sputtering and annealing. For the Ar sputtering, argon gas was dosed into the chamber to an pressure of at a pressure of $5\cdot10^{-5}$~\si{mbar} and the Ar$^+$ ions were accelerated with an voltage of $1$~\si{keV}. The annealing was done at approximately 825~\si{\K} for 45~\si{minutes} to heal defects on the Ag(100) surface. The cleanliness of the Ag(100) surface is verified by \ac{LEED} and \ac{XPS} measurements before the deposition of \ac{QA} molecules.

\section{Experimental setup}

The \ac{XPS} and \ac{NIXSW} measurements were performed at the end-station in EH2 of the I09 beamline at the Diamond Light Source in Didcot, UK.\autocite{Diamond2025} A schematic drawing of the experimental end-station is shown in \autoref{fig:I09}. At the end-station in EH2, an \ac{UHV} chamber with a base pressure of $approx 10^{-10}$~\si{mbar} is available for surface science experiments, equipped with various preparation and analysis tools.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{images/vertiefer/I09.jpg}
	\caption{Schematic drawing of the I09 endstation of the Diamond Light Source in Didcot, UK. Picture taken from reference \cite{Diamond2025}.}
	\label{fig:I09}
\end{figure}

The end-station in EH2 (\autoref{fig:I09}) utilizes synchrotron radiation from an undulator inside the electron storage ring of the Diamond Light Source. At the end-station in EH2, hard ($2.1-18$~\si{keV}) and soft  ($230-2000$~\si{eV}) x-rays are available for the experiments. For the hard x-rays, a double-crystal Si(111) monochromator is used, while for the soft x-rays a plane grating monochromator is employed. The x-rays are then directed into the experimental chamber using different focusing mirrors.\autocite{Diamond2025}

The experimental chamber is equipped with a 5 axes manipulator that allows precise sample positioning in x,y,z direction as well as polar and azimuth angles and contains a cryostat for cooling the sample down with liquid nitrogen or liquid helium. In addition, there are different preparation chambers attached to the experimental chamber for sample preparation, such as \ac{QA} deposition.\autocite{Diamond2025}

For the \ac{XPS} and \ac{NIXSW} measurements, a hemispherical electron energy analyzer (VG Scienta EW4000 HAXPES) with a 70 frame per second CCD camera is used that allows the detection of photoelectrons with kinetic energies up to 10~\si{kV}. The analyzer has an acceptance cone of 56$^\circ$ and the analyzer-incident beam angle is fixed at 90$^\circ$  or 60$^\circ$.\autocite{Diamond2025} The sample geometries for the \ac{XPS} and \ac{NIXSW} measurements are illustrated in \autoref{fig:experimental}.

\section{XPS measurements}

The \ac{XPS} and \ac{NIXSW} measurements of \ac{QA} on Ag(100) were performed with the previously described experimental setup at the I09 endstation of the Diamond Light Source in Didcot, UK. The \ac{XPS} measurements were conducted using photon energies of 500~\si{\eV} for the C1s and N1s core levels and 630~\si{\eV} for the O1s core level. The pass energy of the hemispherical electron energy analyzer was set to 20~\si{\eV}. The step width for the spectra acquisition was set to 40~\si{meV} and 17 frames were recorded for each core level and then averaged. \section{Data processing for the XPS spectra}
\label{sec:data-processing-XPS}

The measured \ac{XPS} spectra were processed and analyzed using the CasaXPS software\autocite{CasaSoftwareLtd2022}. First, the \ac{XPS} spectra with same experimental parameters and same experimental preparation were averaged to increase the signal-to-noise ratio.

Then, the \ac{BE} of the averaged \ac{XPS} spectra were calibrated against the Fermi edge, which was measured for each preparation after the \ac{XPS} measurements of \ac{QA} on the Ag(100) surface. This was done by fitting a \ac{XPS} spectrum of the Fermi edge with the Fermi function

\begin{equation}
	f(E) = \frac{a}{1 + e^{\frac{E - E_F}{k_\mathrm{B}T}}} + b
	\label{eq:fermi}
\end{equation}

where $a$ is the amplitude, $b$ the offset, $E_F$ the Fermi energy, $k_\mathrm{B}$ the Boltzmann constant, and $T=300~\si{K}$ the temperature. The \ac{BE} scale of the \ac{XPS} spectra of \ac{QA} on Ag(100) was then calibrated by aligning the fitted Fermi energy $E_F$ to 0~\si{\eV} accordingly.

After the calibration of the \ac{BE} scale, the background of the \ac{XPS} spectra was subtracted using a linear background or a Shirley background\autocite{Shirley1972} depending on the individual spectrum. 

Finally, the different core level peaks of the \ac{XPS} spectra were fitted using a numerical convolution of Lorentzian and a Gaussian to produce a Voigt profile. This function is called asymmetric Lorentzian lineshape LA($\alpha$,$\beta$,$m$), where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ describe the asymmetry of the peak and $m$ the width of the Gaussian.\autocite{CasaSoftwareLtd2022} The asymmetric Lorentzian lineshape is defined as

\begin{equation}
	LA(\alpha,\beta) = \begin{cases}
		\left[L(x) \right]^\alpha & x \leq E \\
		\left[L(x) \right]^\beta & x > E
	\end{cases}
\end{equation}

with 
\begin{equation}
	L(x) = \frac{1}{1 + 4 \left( \frac{(x - E)}{\text{FWHM}} \right)^2} ~.
\end{equation}

This lineshape was used because it shows the best description of the \ac{XPS} spectra. In addition to the lineshape, the area of the peak can be constrained. This was used to match the number of C atoms in the molecule to the number of C atoms in the corresponding peak. The exact parameters used for the peak fitting in CasaXPS are summarized in \autoref{tab:casa}.

As explained in the previous chapter, the \ac{FWHM} of the peaks in the \ac{XPS} spectra is influenced by various effects, such as instrumental broadening and the lifetime of the photohole. To account for these, the \ac{FWHM} of the different peaks was not constrained to a fixed value, but was allowed to vary within a certain range. The exact values of the \ac{FWHM} for the different peaks are given in the following chapter for each fit. 

\section{NIXSW measurements}

The \ac{NIXSW} measurements were performed with the Ag(200) Bragg reflection. The Bragg energy of the Ag(200) reflection is $E^{200}_\mathrm{Bragg} = 3035.54$~\si{\eV}. For the \ac{NIXSW} measurements, energies of the x-ray beam $E_\mathrm{\gamma}$ around the Bragg energy were used to excite photoelectrons from the C1s, O1s, and N1s core levels. The photon energy $E_\mathrm{\gamma}$ is between $E^{200}_\mathrm{Bragg} - 4.5~\si{eV}$ and $E^{200}_\mathrm{Bragg} + 4.5~\si{eV}$.

The different \ac{NIXSW} spectra were measured at a fresh sample position to avoid radiation damage effects. Furthermore, before an \ac{NIXSW} measurement was performed, the reflectivity curve of the Ag(200) Bragg reflection was recorded to ensure that the position is usable und for the data evaluation. The maximum \ac{FWHM} of the reflectivity curve was set to 0.90~\si{eV}. 

The energy of the x-ray beam can be further classified as the \textit{out-of-Bragg} regions, which is 2.5~\si{eV} below and above the Bragg energy. For these two regions, 4 data points (photoemission spectea) were measured, because there is no standing wavefield in the {out-of-Bragg} region and thus only the photoemission yield of the measured orbitals. The region with an energy of $E^{200}_\mathrm{Bragg} \pm 2.5~\si{eV}$ was measured with 26 data points. The other experimental parameters for the \ac{NIXSW} measurements are summarized in \autoref{tab:experimental_NIXSW}.

\section{Data processing for the NIXSW spectra}

The \ac{NIXSW} data was processed and analyzed with CasaXPS\autocite{CasaSoftwareLtd2022} and Torricelli 4.0.1.\autocite{Bocquet2019}
 
First, all single \ac{XPS} spectra of the \ac{NIXSW} measurement were processed in the same way as described for the \ac{XPS} spectra in the \autoref{sec:data-processing-XPS}, but without the calibration of the \ac{BE} to the Fermi edge and the area constraint. The relative positions and the \ac{FWHM} of the peaks were constrained to the values from the fit model for the \ac{XPS} spectra for all measurements and only the peak areas were allowed to vary. The errors of the integrated peak areas were computed by means of the Monte Carlo-based procedure implemented in CasaXPS\autocite{CasaSoftwareLtd2022} and were used for the error calculation of the \ac{NIXSW} data in Torricelli 4.0.1.\autocite{Bocquet2019}.

Afterwards, the peak areas of the different core levels for each photon energy $E_\mathrm{\gamma}$ were then further processed in Torricelli 4.0.1.\autocite{Bocquet2019}. Torricelli computes the ideal reflectivity curve and the phase of the standing wavefield for the given Bragg plane (here: Ag(200)) for the temperature of the experiment (here: $T=300$~\si{K}) and the Bragg angle (here: ). Then, the experimental relfectivity curve is fitted. Finally, the photoemission yield is fitted with taking the non-dipolar correction parameters from \autoref{tab:non-dipolar} into account to determine the coherent fraction and the coherent position. The normalized errors of the photoemission yield were calculated by a Monte Carlo analysis that is implemented in Torricelli 4.0.1.\autocite{Bocquet2019} The final results of the \ac{NIXSW} measurements are the coherent fraction $f_\mathrm{c}$ and the coherent position $p_\mathrm{c}$ with their respective errors.
