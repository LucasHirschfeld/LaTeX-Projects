\section{Structures of QA on the Ag(100) surface}

\Ac{QA} forms two different phases on the Ag(100) surface. Upon adsorption by room temperature, the molecules arrange in the so-called $\alpha$-phase with a \ac{pol} structure. By annealing the sample to 500~\si{K}, the molecules rearrange into the commensurate $\beta$-phase. These two phases were first observed and characterized by \ac{STM} and \ac{LEED} measurements. All information that are presented in the following is based on the work of Humberg et al. \autocite{Humberg2020, Humberg2025} if not stated otherwise.

As said above, the $\alpha$-phase of \ac{QA} on the Ag(100) surface has forms when \ac{QA} molecules are deposited onto the Ag(100) surface at room temperature. The $\alpha$-phase consists of domains of homochiral molecular chains that are parallel to each other. The \ac{QA} molecules absorb with its $\pi$-system parallel to the surface in a face-on orientation.

The molecular chains are dominantly formed due to intermolecular hydrogen bonds between the H atoms of the amine group of one molecule and the O atoms of the carbonyl group of another molecule. This means that each \ac{QA} molecule has four hydrogen bonds to its two nearest neighbors, resulting in two hydrogen bonds per molecule, as each hydrogen bond is shared between two molecules. 

The evaluation of the \ac{SPA-LEED} pattern of the $\alpha$-phase results in the following unit cell matrix:

\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{M_\alpha} =\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 1.25 \\
-3 & 4.80 
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation*}

which indicates a \ac{pol} structure, because the values of the first column are integers.\autocite{Hooks2001} This indicates the importance of the substrate for the formation of the $\alpha$-phase, as every lattice point of the superstructure matches with a substrate lattice line of the [10] direction.

The unit cell is primitive rectangular ($\alpha=90° \pm 1°$) with the intermolecular distance in one molecular chains of $\mathbf{b}_1=(6.8\pm0.1)~\si{\angstrom}$ and the distance of two parallel molecular chains of $\mathbf{b}_2=(16.4\pm0.1)~\si{\angstrom}$ and contains one \ac{QA} molecule. The \ac{STM} images, the \ac{SPA-LEED} pattern and the structure model of the $\alpha$-phase are shown in Figure \ref{fig:literature_alpha}.

When the sample is annealed to 500~\si{K}, the molecules rearrange irreversibly into the thermodynamically favored $\beta$-phase. This means that the $\alpha$-phase is a metastable phase, which is kinetically stabilized by e.g. the intermolecular hydrogen bonds. The exact mechanism of the phase transition is not known.

Similar to the $\alpha$-phase, the \ac{QA} molecules in the $\beta$-phase also adsorb with its $\pi$-system parallel to the surface in a face-on orientation and in molecular chains that are parallel to each other. In contrast to the $\alpha$-phase, the molecular chains are not homochiral, but consist of dimers with alternating handedness. In addition, the molecular chains also exhibit periodic indents.

This leads to a diffferent situation of the hydrogen bonds. The two \ac{QA} molecules of a dimer are connected by two hydrogen bonds, but the dimers are only connected by one hydrogen bond due to the indents. This means that each \ac{QA} molecule has three hydrogen bonds to its two nearest neighbors, resulting in 1.5 hydrogen bonds per molecule and an energy loss due to the missing hydrogen bonds.

The evaluation of the \ac{SPA-LEED} pattern of the $\beta$-phase results in the following unit cell matrix:

\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{M_\beta} =\begin{pmatrix}
4 & 3 \\
-5 & 3 
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation*}

which corresponds to a commensurate structure. This indicates strong substrate interactions and is related to generally related to a gain in energy, which compensates the energy loss due to the missing hydrogen bonds.\autocite{Hooks2001} It is important to note that the unit cell of the $\beta$-phase does not take the handedness of the molecules into account, as the unit cell is determined by \ac{SPA-LEED}, which is not sensitive to the handedness of the molecules, becuase the enantiomers have very similar structure factors.

The unit cell of the $\beta$-phase is oblique ($\alpha=112.2° \pm 1°$) with the unit cell vectors $\mathbf{b}_1=(6.9\pm0.1)~\si{\angstrom}$ and $\mathbf{b}_2=(15.8\pm0.1)~\si{\angstrom}$ and contains two \ac{QA} molecules. The \ac{STM} images, the \ac{SPA-LEED} pattern and the structure model of the $\beta$-phase are shown in Figure \ref{fig:literature_beta}.

In addition, it was investigated if the $\beta$-phase involves metal-coordinated bonds between the \ac{QA} molecules, which could be dismissed.\autocite{Humberg2025,Bald2021}

Further questions regarding the two phases are still open, e.g. why the thermodynamically favored $\beta$-phase does not form directly upon adsorption at room temperature, and what the exact mechanism of the phase transition is.
