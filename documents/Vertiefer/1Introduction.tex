\chapter{Introduction}

The interface of organic semiconductors with metal surfaces is of particular importance within the domain of organic electronics. The aforementioned organic electronics include, for example, \acp{OLED}, \acp{OPV} and \acp{OFET}.\autocite{Koch2007}

One organic molecule that has been extensively studied in the context of organic electronics is \acf{QA}. \Ac{QA} is an organic, prochiral molecule with a large $\pi$-conjugated system, which is known for its use as a dye in printer toners and varnishes.\autocite{Hye2012} However, its potential extends beyond the domain of dyes, as previous studies demonstrated the application of \ac{QA} in organic electronics.\autocite{Glowacki2013, DanielGlowacki2012, Wang2016} Specific applications for \ac{QA} have been identified, including its use in \acp{OLED}\autocite{Wang2016a, Min2021, Cunha2018}, \acp{OPV}\autocite{Dunst2017, Sung2017} and \acp{OFET}\autocite{Jeon2018, Jeong2017, Kanbur2019, Berg2009}. In addition, \ac{QA} has been shown to form well-defined structures on metal surfaces, such as the Ag(100), the Ag(111) and the Cu(111) surface.\autocite{Humberg2020,Humberg2024,Humberg2023,Humberg2024a,Wagner2014}

A common structural motif of \ac{QA} monolayers on surfaces\autocite{Humberg2024, Wagner2014, Trixler2007, Eberle2019} and in its bulk structure\autocite{Paulus2007} are 1D molecular chains. The formation of the 1D molecular chains is driven by intermolecular hydrogen bonds.\autocite{Humberg2024, Humberg2020}

In particular, \ac{QA} on the Ag(100) surface forms two different phases, the $\alpha$-phase and the $\beta$-phase. The $\alpha$-phase consists of the previously described homochiral, parallel lying 1D molecular chains. This leads to a \ac{POL} structure for the $\alpha$-phase. In contrast, the $\beta$-phase is characterized by a commensurate phase, which is thermodynamically more stable than the $\alpha$-phase and forms irreversibly when the sample is heated. The $\beta$-phase has fewer hydrogen bonds and stronger adsorbate-substrate interactions. In addition to this, the $\beta$-phase has proposed to be heterochiral, necessitating a change of the handedness of the molecules during the phase transition.\autocite{Humberg2024, Humberg2020}

The phase transition and the differences between the two phases are of particular interest, as the change in the binding situation of the different atoms are not yet known.

The present study is focused on how the binding situation of \ac{QA} on the Ag(100) surface is impacted by the interplay between intermolecular interactions via hydrogen bonds and adsorbate-substrate interactions across the two distinct phases. Specifically, the quantity of the hydrogen bonds and the specific atoms that are involved in the adsorbate-surface interactions are of interest. Furthermore, the diversity of chemical environments that exist for the different atoms in the molecule and the subsequent alterations that occur upon phase transition, are of particular relevance.

For this purpose, \ac{XPS} is used as the primary technique. \Ac{XPS} provides insights into the binding energies of the core level electrons for the different types of atoms present in the adsorbate. Consequently, the binding energies of the core level electrons give information about the binding situation of the different substrate atoms on the Ag(100) surface. However, the details about the binding situation of the adsorbate are not accessible with the previously used techniques, such as \ac{STM} and \ac{LEED}, as they only provide information about the molecular arrangement.

The \ac{XPS} spectra offer new insights regarding the priorly mentioned binding situation. Previous \ac{STM} and \ac{LEED} studies proposed 2 hydrogen bonds per molecule in the $\alpha$-phase and 1.5 hydrogen bonds per molecule in the $\beta$-phase.\autocite{Humberg2024, Humberg2020} Similarly, the present study determined 2 hydrogen bonds per molecule in the $\alpha$-phase, but in contrast to previous studies, only 1 hydrogen bond per molecule in the $\beta$-phase. This finding necessitates a new structure model, a subject that will be examined in the subsequent chapters.
